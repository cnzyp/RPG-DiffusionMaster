To the Editors
Pattern Recognition

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "The Injection Site Determines What
Identity Survives in Training-Free Multi-Concept Personalization," for publication in Pattern Recognition.

Training-free methods for generating an image containing several specific,
user-supplied concepts all impose a spatial constraint somewhere in the
diffusion process, and they differ in where.  That choice is almost never
isolated from the others made alongside it, so the literature reports
architectures rather than mechanisms.  This manuscript isolates it: the
backbone, the adapter, the injection scale, the layout planner and the seed
are held fixed, and only the injection site varies.  The sites form a ladder,
and what each rung costs is measured rather than argued.

Three results seem to us worth the journal's attention.

  1. Masking an image adapter inside cross-attention -- the most widely used
     construction in this setting -- recovers 1.7% of the identity the same
     adapter delivers from the sampling loop, an amount indistinguishable
     from zero, and no injection strength repairs it.  We show this is not a
     tuning failure but a consequence of what a convolutional backbone
     entangles.

  2. Similarity to a reference cannot distinguish a weak conditioning signal
     from a dead one, and we give a control that can: swap a region's
     reference for another instance of the same category and ask whether the
     output follows.  A reference-free baseline sits at exactly zero by
     construction.  Under this test one widely used configuration rewrites
     the image substantially while moving toward its newly assigned
     reference by nothing (p = 0.79).

  3. Identity gain and face area correlate at r = 0.949 across seven methods,
     which means published identity scores in this area are partly a
     measurement of framing.  We report both quantities throughout and give
     a framing-independent alternative.

The endpoint of the ladder is a complete method that, on a frozen backbone and
at 0.83x the cost per image, leads a baseline trained on a subject-annotated
corpus on identity and on concept isolation, ties it on placement, and trails
it on text alignment by 0.004.  We release the Mixed-Eval-v1 benchmark (150
two- and three-region samples) and the full evaluation harness.

The work is methodological and empirical in the way Pattern Recognition
publishes: a controlled comparison, a measurement instrument for a property
the existing metrics conflate, and negative results reported with the size of
each cost attached.

Pattern Recognition asks three questions of every cover letter.

Was the study compared to the state of the art?  Yes.  Six methods are run
under one protocol, on the same hardware, prompts and seeds: RPG, IP-Adapter,
IP-Adapter-FaceID, Conceptrol, an attention-gated regional adapter, and
MS-Diffusion.  Five articles presenting the state of the art on this topic:

  1. Wang et al., MS-Diffusion: Multi-subject Zero-shot Image Personalization
     with Layout Guidance, ICLR 2025.

  2. He et al., Conceptrol: Concept Control of Zero-shot Personalized Image
     Generation, 2025.

  3. Yu et al., AnyMS: Bottom-up Attention Decoupling for Layout-guided and
     Training-free Multi-subject Generation, 2025.

  4. Li et al., PhotoMaker: Customizing Realistic Human Photos via Stacked ID
     Embedding, CVPR 2024.

  5. Wang et al., InstantID: Zero-shot Identity-Preserving Generation in
     Seconds, 2024.

The first two are run as baselines from the authors' own code.  AnyMS has no
public implementation -- checked on its arXiv page and by search -- so it is
discussed rather than run; it matters because it is built on the same SDXL
and IP-Adapter pairing to which our negative result is scoped.  PhotoMaker
and InstantID are single-subject and identity-specific, so they bound the
problem rather than solve it, and the manuscript says where.

Which public datasets exist for this problem, and which were used?
DreamBench++ (Peng et al., ICLR 2025) is the public benchmark for
personalized generation, and we use it: Supplementary S6 evaluates on
DreamBench-2, 150 two-concept samples built from DreamBench++ references over
30 concept pairs.  DreamBooth's set is single-subject and cannot express a
multi-region task.  Because no public benchmark pairs several specific
subjects with a layout, we construct and release two: Mixed-Eval-v1 (150 two-
and three-region samples across all categories) and Scaling-Bench (90
samples, N = 4, 5 and 6).

Which measures does the validation framework use, and which did we use?  The
standard set is DINO and CLIP-I for subject fidelity, CLIP-T for prompt
adherence, FID or KID for realism, and ArcFace cosine similarity for faces.
We report DINO-ID, CLIP-T, ArcFace, ArcFace-P, FID and KID.  We add three the
standard set cannot express, and argue for each in the manuscript: a leakage
margin, since similarity to the correct reference means nothing without the
distance to the wrong one; region-level binding accuracy; and a
reference-swap response, because similarity cannot tell a weak conditioning
signal from a dead one.  FID and KID are reported for completeness and are
explicitly not criteria here -- the reference set is single-subject
photographs while every method is asked for a multi-subject scene, so both
reward failing the task.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not
under consideration elsewhere, in whole or in part.  The authors have no competing
interests to declare, and received no specific funding for this work.
